01. THE PROJECT AND THE CURRENT DELIVERABLE

You are designing the interface for a Naruto-universe idle squad RPG. Create a fresh native Android presentation project using Kotlin and Jetpack Compose. The current deliverable is a collection of fully designed game screens with believable illustration, precise hierarchy, every intended control drawn, and complete visual state examples.

The owner wants the Naruto world to be immediately recognizable. Use Naruto Shippuden-era Hidden Leaf Village as the visual foundation. Include Konoha architecture, Hokage Rock, familiar shinobi clothing, forehead protectors, the Leaf emblem, ninja tools, sealing scrolls, training grounds, mission desks and chakra imagery. “The Broken Supply Route” is the opening campaign arc inside this broader Naruto setting. Design the Home, Hall, Shop and other destinations as places within that world.

AFK Arena and Mythic Heroes are references for game-interface composition. Study the city of buildings, prominent character portraits, compact inventory tiles, readable resource bars and well-defined actions. Render those ideas through Naruto’s architecture, colors, costumes and objects. Create a deliberate mobile game interface with its own arrangement of these elements.

This is VISUAL DESIGN ONLY. Render gameplay buttons in the intended finished appearance, including normal, pressed, selected, disabled and locked specimens. Their gameplay onClick handlers remain absent or empty. Selecting Train, Recruit, Buy, Battle, Claim, Equip, Join or Save must produce no game action. Render confirmations, results and errors as separate predefined screens. Use static example data. There is no combat engine, wallet arithmetic, reward grant, save system, upgrade progression, network account, payment integration, AI service or production notification integration in this phase.

One temporary review selector may switch the displayed screen and its visual variant. That selector belongs to a review frame outside the game screen. It is the only navigation needed to inspect the work. Scrolling long static pages and expanding the review frame are presentation conveniences. Game tabs, buildings and buttons can remain inert in the screenshots. Draw their complete visual affordances and document their intended destinations for later implementation.

Use stable screen IDs from this document. Every screen must have an individually inspectable preview. Every category receives a real composition. Generic cards headed “Hall,” “Shop” or “Arena” do not satisfy the location design.

Use the master’s current category structure, later decisions and stated unknowns. The master defines systems and many information requirements; this document supplies the new visual design. Treat named Naruto characters as visual casting examples. Their assignment to the master’s K-01–K-19 combat contracts remains a later decision. A Naruto portrait, village badge, chakra effect or shop tile must not silently establish a skill, faction bonus, rarity rule, product or economy value.

02. THE VISUAL WORLD

Era and tone. Use the recognizable Shippuden silhouette language: layered green tiled roofs, rounded plaster structures, timber storefronts, red-orange architectural details, cylindrical towers, overhead pipes, railings, wooden signs, fabric curtains and leafy streets. Konoha feels busy, lived in and capable of ordinary village life. The Academy yard is open and sunlit. Mission interiors are organized and paper-filled. Dangerous routes become dusk forests, ravines and misted roads. Reserve the darkest palette for hostile encounters and specific character art.

Art style. Polished anime game illustration with accurate Naruto facial proportions, clean silhouette edges, controlled cel shadows and detailed environment painting. Use material detail on roofs, wood, cloth, headbands and weapons. Faces must stay readable at small size. Avoid a photograph-like face pasted onto an anime body. Show full bodies in formation and battle, face-and-shoulder crops in roster tiles and large waist-up or full-body art in dossiers.

Recurring cast for visual specimens. Naruto has spiky blond hair, orange-and-black Shippuden clothes, headband and an energetic forward pose. Sasuke has dark hair, open light top and rope-belt silhouette. Sakura has pink hair, a clear red/pink outfit silhouette and purposeful hands. Kakashi has silver hair, mask, headband and green flak vest. Shikamaru, Hinata, Gaara and Tenten supply additional distinct face, costume and stance examples. Keep an era-consistent appearance for each character across every screen. Use one approved portrait sheet per character as the reference for all crops. Stage the same five sample characters consistently when demonstrating a full team.

Materials. Main navigation uses deep navy cloth and gunmetal edging reminiscent of shinobi equipment. Information surfaces use pale mission parchment and fine ink rules. Primary actions use warm orange lacquer framed by a narrow darker edge. Secondary actions use cream paper or indigo cloth. Roster and item frames use restrained metal corners and a clear selected outline. A real scroll panel may have rolled ends, a tie or a folded edge. Do not put a full decorative scroll around every sentence.

Color starting points. Naruto orange #F07C2B, night navy #192839, Leaf green #577B48, roof jade #3F6F5D, parchment #F5E6C9, ink #25303A, steel #B3BDC2, village vermilion #AA493B and light text #FFF6E7. Chakra uses a contained cyan-blue accent #54B9DB. Purple is reserved for relevant genjutsu or character contexts. Selection uses orange plus an outline. Locks use a padlock plus a written condition. Damage, healing and shielding each have distinct symbols as well as color.

Typography. Use a sturdy, readable display face for short destination names and chapter titles. Use a clean sans-serif for numbers, body copy and controls. Default specimen sizes are 26–30sp title, 20–22sp panel heading, 15–16sp body, 13–14sp supporting text and 15–17sp button label. Keep numbers tabular where available. English is the initial display language. Use Japanese words or signs only when the exact supplied text is verified. Decorative unreadable pseudo-Japanese must not carry navigation.

04. CANVAS, ASSETS AND SHARED COMPONENTS

Use a 390×844dp portrait artboard as the comfortable design reference. Inspect the same composition at 360×640dp and 320×568dp. Respect actual system insets. Measure regions within the safe content area rather than hardcoding a phone status bar. Keep ordinary horizontal gutters near 16dp, 8dp between close components and 16–24dp between sections. Large illustrations may bleed to the edge; readable controls keep a safe inset.

The root header is approximately 56dp. Its left portrait or Back anchor takes a 48dp touch envelope. A root resource strip displays at most two essential currencies and one wallet/details affordance. The root navigation is approximately 72dp plus system inset. Four equal destinations have a 24–28dp symbol, written label and selected plate. Leave enough space for a thumb without covering an item’s cost. Long pages scroll. At 200% text, stack formerly horizontal information and keep actions visible by scrolling instead of shrinking type.

Reusable design specimens to build before expanding screens:

C01 Primary action. Orange lacquer rectangle, subtly clipped or tapered corners, 52dp reference height, one dark lower edge and centered cream label. Optional small leading glyph. Pressed state lowers the highlight and darkens the face. Disabled state reduces saturation and includes a clear reason nearby. The visual style should remain readable over a busy background.
C02 Secondary action. Cream surface, dark ink label, fine navy border, same height. Use for Details, Cancel, Compare or secondary inspection. Tertiary action is a text label with a small consistent chevron.
C03 Header and Back. Short destination name, left-facing arrow with the parent name available, optional single utility on the right. Keep Back distinct from a Close X.
C04 Building plaque. Small wooden or cloth-backed nameplate tied to a building’s silhouette. Selected specimen adds a subtle edge highlight around the building and a brighter plaque. Locked specimen adds a padlock and short condition. The same building must still be recognizable without the plaque.
C05 Portrait card. Reference 86×118dp at 390dp width, face occupying the top two-thirds, short name below, one role or village glyph, and room for a selected/equipped marker. Maintain face crop and eye level. Avoid five stacked badges over the face.
C06 Item tile. Three columns at normal size. A square 88–100dp art cell, one quantity at lower-right, type/quality cue in the border, a price strip below if it is stock. Two-column or list variants support larger text.
C07 Resource chip. Specific resource icon, readable amount and full label in an expanded specimen. Ryo uses a coin, EXP a training scroll, fragments a clearly broken portrait/seal piece, Recruitment Seals a closed document seal. Keep resource silhouettes distinct.
C08 Tabs and filters. Cloth or paper tabs with a strong active underline and label. Horizontal tabs have a short, finite set. A filter sheet shows selected tick marks, Reset and Apply specimens. Sorting has a visible current criterion.
C09 Progress rail. A labeled start, present marker, endpoint and the exact meaning of the fill. Hall progress, chapter progress and collection milestones use distinct captions. Pending uses a separate state label.
C10 Character stage. Transparent character cutout or field sprite over a separate location plate, ground shadow and native name/indicator layer. Preserve head, hand and weapon within crop-safe bounds.
C11 Formation pad. Five consistent slot identities. Selected outline, occupant name, role icon and one Open variant. The pad stays visible under feet and matches the later battle position.
C12 Status token. Wound cut mark, Silence crossed speech seal, Stun impact/star mark, Mark target tag, shield ward outline, Taunt attention hook and cleanse dissolving paper tag. Include a label and remaining-duration/trigger specimen.
C13 Item detail sheet. Enlarged art above name/type, relevant properties, ownership/location, price or requirement and one dominant action. Avoid hiding a material difference below the button.
C14 Confirmation. Title, exact object, before/after or cost area, consequence, primary action and Cancel. Controls sit at the end of the readable content.
C15 Message state. Compact icon, plain-language status and one next-action specimen. Prepare loading, empty, locked, unavailable, pending, failed, completed and already-owned treatments.
C16 Reward presentation. A small set of enlarged reward objects on a parchment tray, clearly separated amount labels and an already-collected variation. Reserve a tall character reveal for a first-copy portrait.
C17 Modal and bottom sheet. Darkened backdrop, opaque readable content, visible Close/Back, safe margins, bounded height with vertical scroll and a static focus-ring specimen. Avoid transparency behind long text.
C18 Tooltip and glossary. Small anchored paper note for one short explanation; full dossier for longer rules. Icon-only meaning must also appear as text.
C19 Notification marker. One small dot or count plus a clear state in its expanded view. No pulsing field of alerts. Critical pending-result appearance is distinct from an optional new cosmetic.
C20 Review frame. Screen ID, category and variant controls outside the artwork; render one game screen at a time. Include a full-screen clean view for screenshots and a contact-sheet option if the tooling supports one.

Use separate background art, character cutouts, icon assets, decorative frames and native text. Source images must be local or bundled. Every art asset needs a descriptive key, intended screen, aspect ratio, focal anchor and crop-safe area. Example keys are bg_konoha_village, bg_academy_training_yard, bg_ninja_tool_shop, bg_hokage_mission_office, map_supply_route, bg_chunin_arena, bg_scroll_archive and portrait_kakashi_shippuden.

For a vertical environment, begin with a 9:16 painting at roughly 1080×1920 or a comparable size and leave flexible top/bottom crop. Hero portraits need at least a 3:4 source and a consistent face crop. Field characters need separate transparent full-body poses at a consistent camera angle. Icons should remain readable at 24dp, with larger exports for item art. Do not stretch a single landscape image into portrait. Do not use a generated screenshot as an entire functioning screen. Compose the layers and text separately.

Static preview values are specimens, held in one presentation-data source. Example review balances may be 1,280 Ryo, 12 Seals and 45 Fragments, with “Sample data” shown in the review frame. These amounts are not a new economy. Unknown shop prices, rates and late-game requirements use a labeled sample or “To be defined” in the relevant design specimen. A displayed success is a separate preview state. It never changes another screen’s balance.

Confirmation/cancel — affected object, consequence and two unambiguous actions.

Shared overlays include cost breakdown, material shortage, image/portrait zoom, skill tooltip, item source, reward receipt, generic confirmation, Back/Close, profile edit, sort/filter, support report and a neutral error sheet. Use screenshots to inspect overlap and typography. A state is a separate preview, never a simulated transaction or active background process.

09. WHAT COUNTS AS FINISHED UI

For each screen, record its ID, name, category, main source section, art status, completed visual variants and screenshot path. Use the existing project conventions where possible. No screen is finished merely because its function name exists.

A finished screen has its specified location or subject art, native text and controls, intentional spacing, meaningful state examples, coherent parent/header treatment and a clean small-phone composition. Every price-like field has a visible currency and a defined specimen status. Every action has a visible label or a clear icon with a label in its associated specimen. Every detailed sheet has an obvious Close/Back design. Character faces, rank/ownership marks and item counts are legible at their final size.

Make a button/overlay inventory in the design notes. For each control, name its visual appearance, intended later destination or action, and the static state used now. This documents the clickable surface without implementing the behavior. Include the building plaques and their exact corresponding location screens.

Run an Android build/preview check where available, plus visual inspection of actual rendered output. A UI-only phase does not need game-system tests. Verify that game controls have no external side effects and that only review selection/scrolling changes what is visible. If screenshots cannot be captured, give precise preview-selection steps and state that the rendering is unverified.

Village — can I identify Konoha, the Academy/Hall and the Shop before reading their labels?
Hall — can I see a training location, the shared level, the next cost panel and the Train control without searching?
Shop — can I see a Naruto tool merchant’s space, identify each product and read the sample price/action?

Review questions for the full set:

Can I distinguish earned, equipped, selected, locked and pending by label/shape as well as color?
