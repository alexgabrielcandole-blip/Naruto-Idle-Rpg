34. Resource sinks, upgrade-cost structure and progression
pacing (Version 0.10.5)
Purpose and status. This chapter connects the Section 33 resource-source map to how the game spends
resources. It establishes a first-pass sink register, describes how costs should behave across early, mid and late
progression, and defines the accounting required before exact values can be chosen. All Naruto Idle RPG
mechanics in this chapter are [PROPOSAL] or [RESEARCH SYNTHESIS], not owner-approved final rules.
Benchmark numbers are examples from named games and must not be copied as our economy. No
implementation, economy simulator, seeded battle run or player test was performed.

34.1 Research questions and evidence rules
   A resource source (Section 33) answers “where does it enter the account?” A resource sink answers “what
    consumes it, and for what player-facing benefit?” A stock is the amount currently held. A recurring
    income rate must be distinguished from one-time first-clear or milestone rewards.
   Every resource family needs a primary purpose, allowed secondary uses, a dependable acquisition path, a
    restriction / expiry rule where relevant, and a guardrail against becoming either worthless or an
    unavoidable hard gate.
   “Mandatory” means the resource is required for baseline team functionality or ordinary progression.
    “Optimization” means it improves efficiency or options but is not required to keep a viable team
    functioning. The same resource may serve both, but its baseline and advanced uses must be separated
    clearly.
   Keep three evidence layers separate: source-derived benchmark observations; project-specific design
    proposals; and unresolved values that require later owner decisions or validation. No benchmark source
    establishes the optimal cost curve for our game.

34.2 What the benchmark evidence supports
AFK Arena — multiple resource tracks and milestone costs. A community-maintained guide published 21
October 2026 reports that hero leveling consumes Gold and Hero EXP regularly, with Hero’s Essence charged
at selected breakpoints tied to skill upgrades. Its example cost for one hero from level 1 to 100 is 1,953,229
Gold, 1,088,586 Hero EXP and 900 Essence; it reports still steeper EXP costs later. The same guide describes
focusing level investment on five heroes and using the Resonating Crystal to share levels with additional
heroes. This is useful structural evidence of separate cost tracks, milestone gates and shared-level
infrastructure, not an economy target for Naruto Idle RPG. The page is community-maintained, not an official
publisher specification, and current values remain patch-sensitive [IR23].
AFK Arena — reversible early investment. The same guide describes free hero resets before campaign stage 4-
36 and a diamond cost after that point. The transferable lesson is not the exact stage or fee; it is that early
experimentation can be made low-risk, then the game can teach players to make more considered
investments after they have learned the systems. A refund or respec policy for our game remains an open
design choice [IR23].
AFK Journey — revise reward supply when the cost structure changes. In a first-party seasonal designer
communication, Farlight Games described moving some upgrade materials earlier in a season, replacing
random equipment drops with Select Equipment Chests, and increasing hero-upgrade material drops after the
new feature raised development costs. This is direct evidence that a cost-system change should trigger a

34.3 Proposed design principles for the Naruto idle RPG
   Design costs and income together. Do not define a resource’s idle or active supply before listing the
    upgrades that consume it; do not define costs without checking whether dependable income can cover the
    intended progression path.
   Protect the functional baseline. The first useful version of a character’s role, core counter or synergy
    should work without a high-rank skill, rare random material, limited-event item or expensive duplicate.
    Advanced investments may improve reliability, efficiency, durability or flexibility, but should not sell
    access to a required tactical function.
   Make the major costs legible. If a promotion, skill unlock or equipment tier requires a large payment,
    signal the threshold and the benefit before the player commits. Avoid hidden cost jumps and undisclosed
    expiration.
   Avoid redundant taxes. A single basic currency may be shared by several systems, but do not layer a fee
    onto every upgrade by default. Each added cost must have a clear economic purpose and be included in
    the time-to-afford calculation.
   Minimize regret during early learning. Evaluate a low-cost reset, refund or transferable investment for
    baseline levels and replaceable starter gear. This is a proposal for discussion, not a locked refund rule.
   Let active modes specialize rewards without monopolizing essentials. A boss, tower, PvP tier, guild or
    short event may improve access to specialized or advanced materials; if a material becomes required for
    basic team function, provide a dependable alternate route or a baseline allowance.
   Keep the idle promise honest. Core idle income should support progress during a campaign wall or
    ordinary absence. It need not replace every active mode or produce every premium, summoning,
    competitive and high-end optimization resource.

34.6 Equipment-cost architecture and replacement regret
   Separate obtaining an item from improving it. Campaign milestones can provide starter / common items;
    targeted gear activities and selectors can handle later needs. Random drops may add variety, but should
    not be the only practical path to a required early slot.
   Do not assume every enhancement should be invested fully. If early gear is expected to be replaced often,
    keep early upgrade cost low, make enhancement transfer possible, or return part of the investment
    through salvage. Pick one clear policy rather than mixing several opaque refund rules.
   Keep high-end set completion and stat refinement as optional optimization until the rest of the equipment
    economy is specified. Do not set an equipment-tier count, per-slot price, rarity curve, enhancement
    success chance, or salvage ratio in this research pass.
   If a future gear system uses random rolls or randomized crafting, separately assess the cost of a failed
    attempt, bad-luck protection, and whether the player can see expected effort. Failure that destroys scarce
    materials can multiply the effective sink; it should not be introduced by default.

34.8 Paper affordability model before any numeric balance pass
For each resource r and planning window W, calculate dependable supply separately from one-off rewards:
RecurringSupply_r(W) = Idle_r(W) + RepeatableActive_r(W) + RecurringObjectives_r(W) +
ScheduledEventBaseline_r(W)
One-time campaign first-clears and unique milestones should be recorded in a separate column, not silently
included in recurring monthly income. Random drops should be shown as variable income, not the only
dependable source of a required resource.
A first-pass affordability estimate for a multi-resource goal G is:
TimeToAfford(G) = max over required resources r of max(0, Cost_r(G) - Balance_r) / ReliableIncomePerDay_r
This is an accounting tool, not a simulator result. It assumes a defined target player profile and reliable
income rates; if a required resource has no dependable path, the estimate is effectively unbounded and that
dependency must be reviewed. When several upgrades compete for the same resource, the budget must
include their shared demand rather than treating all income as freely available to every goal.
   Budget windows to prepare in the next pass: seven days for early upgrade milestones; 28 days for a
    recurring progression and recruitment budget; and one campaign checkpoint interval to distinguish
    recurring supply from first-clear rewards. These are proposed accounting windows, not finalized player-
    session expectations.
   Prepare at least three theoretical profiles: idle-focused; regular active; and highly engaged. The
    comparison asks whether each profile can make useful progress, what optional acceleration active play
    provides, and whether extra engagement creates an unfair competitive gap. No player outcomes should
    be claimed from paper calculations.

34.11 Interim conclusion and next research task
Interim conclusion [RESEARCH SYNTHESIS]: the current direction is a narrow set of recurring core-growth
sinks, specialized but accessible secondary sinks, clear milestone costs and an explicit separation between
baseline functionality and optional optimization. Costs, reliable income and progression windows must be co-
designed and tracked in a paper budget. The AFK Arena example shows why several resources and milestone
costs can be tightly coupled; AFK Journey’s documented seasonal changes show that increased costs should
trigger a review of drop rates and reward certainty. These are design lessons, not a prescription to copy either
game.
Status: the first paper cost-versus-income framework and roster-progression integration are now recorded in
Section 35 (Version 0.10.6). No actual game prices or rates are locked. Next research task: reconcile the core-
progression budget with standard summons, universal fragments, featured-banner cadence and the
unresolved recurring-pull targets; then extend the same budget to active-mode, event and endgame sinks.

35.1 New research questions
   For each major upgrade, how many separate resources must be present at once, and which resource is
    expected to be the limiting factor?
   How much of a player’s dependable daily or weekly income should go toward essential team
    development, and how much should remain available for choice and specialization?
   Can a free-to-play player use a newly acquired tank, healer, debuffer or counter without paying the full
    cost of rebuilding a character from level one?
   How should the economy change from first-session onboarding to midgame roster-building and endgame
    optimization without making the resource curve feel like arbitrary exponential inflation?

35.2 Benchmark findings and evidence limits
Finding A [SOURCE FINDING; specialist guide]. AFK Global’s leveling guide, published 21 October 2026,
describes AFK Arena as charging Gold and Hero EXP on every level, with Hero’s Essence concentrated at
specific level breakpoints that also unlock skill ranks. Its published totals make the relative cost growth
visible: one hero from levels 1-100 is listed at about 1.95 million Gold, 1.09 million EXP and 900 Essence; the
200-240 band is listed at about 21.80 million Gold, 76.46 million EXP and 70,000 Essence. The page is a current
specialist/community guide rather than a publisher-owned economy specification, so use those values as a
documented example, not as a universal benchmark [ECO1].
Design implication [INFERENCE]. The limiting resource can change by progression band: EXP may dominate
ordinary growth while a threshold material gates a skill unlock. A single “upgrade power” score is therefore
insufficient; the paper budget must forecast each resource separately and identify the slowest requirement.
Finding B [SOURCE FINDING; official publisher communication for a specific season]. AFK Journey’s Talk with
Designers: New Season Preview describes replacing random AFK-stage equipment drops with Select
Equipment Chests, moving several one-time stage rewards into a seasonal handbook, making some
progression materials available earlier, and increasing hero-upgrade material drop rates because the updated
development systems would increase hero-development costs [ECO2]. This records one seasonal change, not
an evergreen rule for all versions.
Design implication [INFERENCE]. When a new system increases the cost of development, evaluate its supply
routes and timing in the same design pass. Choice rewards can reduce bad-luck frustration for important
equipment slots, while one-time milestones should not be counted as sustainable recurring income.

35.8 Resource safeguards and edge-case audit
   Resource dominance: if one resource controls almost every upgrade, lowering its income can stop the
    entire game. Keep the dominant bottleneck visible and give it dependable sources; avoid hidden multi-
    resource tolls at every step.
   Parallel commitments: do not promise the same EXP or Ryo stream to leveling, gear enhancement, skill
    ranks and shop purchases simultaneously. Mark core commitments and optional spending separately in
    the budget.
   One-time reward illusion: starter grants and first-clears may make early affordability look healthy.
    Recalculate after those rewards end to confirm the recurring economy can sustain repeat needs.

   New roster acquisition: simulate on paper a player pulling a tank or cleanse counter after the main team

   Equipment replacement: consider a player replacing a starter weapon soon after spending enhancement

   Low engagement / campaign wall: use the guaranteed idle floor and accessible recurring routes, not

   Cost increase after a feature change: any new upgrade tier must trigger a paired review of supply,

    compensation or transition protection is needed.
   F2P and payer comparison: optional spending may shorten waits or broaden convenience, but it must not

35.10 Interim conclusion and next research task
Interim conclusion [RESEARCH SYNTHESIS]. The game should budget progression in time-to-afford, not only

hours full the first claim yields 4,320 EXP and covers all three inputs on day 3. That rule raises income across
the idle basket on every qualifying long absence. A lower Hall price or a targeted timely grant has a narrower
effect.

payment dates from this ledger. The early daily case meets the 1–2 day planning window. The mature daily
promotion takes three days, and the mature stalled two-visit case exceeds the 2–3 day window. These
