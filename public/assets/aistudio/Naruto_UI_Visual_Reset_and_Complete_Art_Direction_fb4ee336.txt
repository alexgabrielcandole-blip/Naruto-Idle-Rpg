03 — REFERENCE TRANSLATION AND ART DIRECTION

AFK Arena reference lesson: important buildings occupy substantial, separately readable areas of the town; character and item silhouettes stay strong; illustrated controls belong to the same world as their backgrounds.
Mythic Heroes reference lesson: a character can own the main stage while stats and actions form a stable lower band; catalog goods repeat within an orderly grid with clear price rows.
Naruto reference lesson: recognizable people, architecture, costumes, weapons, forehead protectors, training grounds, scrolls and chakra provide the actual setting.

These are composition observations from inspected historical reference screenshots. Do not import the references' five-tab navigation, side-promotion density, medieval castles, currencies, monetization, rarity ladders or game rules.

Chosen direction: a polished Shippuden-era Hidden Leaf RPG with confidently drawn anime characters, painterly but controlled environments, custom illustrated object icons and restrained tactile frames. It should feel like a deliberate game art production: faces are consistent, objects have clean edges, architecture has stable perspective and the UI has a coherent material palette.

World:
- Hidden Leaf Village is the main home.
- The Academy training yard is the Training Hall.
- A timber ninja-tool shop serves goods and appearance studies.
- The mission office, scroll registry, archive, storehouse and village gate are distinct places.
- The Broken Supply Route is the opening campaign arc, not a replacement for the Naruto setting.
- Arena uses a Chunin-exam visual language; guild spaces use an operations hall; practice uses the archive.
- Familiar Naruto cast may appear as visual specimens. Do not finalize a named character's mechanical K-ID role, jutsu, rarity or faction advantage through an illustration.

Art hierarchy:
1. Main focal object: the chosen building, character, squad or product. Crisp contour, full material differentiation, strongest local contrast.
2. Useful supporting environment: medium contrast, grouped shapes, simplified texture.
3. Distant background: softened value range and edges, lower saturation, no tiny high-contrast details competing with labels.
4. Interface: stable surfaces, native text, exact alignment and consistent icon scale.

Environmental style:
Use broad light/shadow groups and selective detail. Roof planes should be clean enough to recognize their shape at 390dp width. Paint shutters, pipes and timber joints where they explain architecture. Do not fill every wall with equal microtexture. Make paths broad, shadows directional and entrances visible. Daylight is warm-neutral; foliage and jade roofs remain distinct. Limit dramatic fog to appropriate mission scenes.

Character style:
Preserve Naruto anime proportions, recognizable facial structure, costume era and silhouette. Use a consistent model sheet. Keep spiky hair, headbands, hands, straps and weapons anatomically coherent. Avoid waxy faces, generic fantasy armor, random kimono substitutions or mismatched body rendering. Dossier art may show a waist-up crop; field art must show the body and grounded feet. Do not stretch a portrait to make a battle sprite.

Shape vocabulary:
Rectangular mission paper, slightly clipped lacquered buttons, compact steel brackets, folded cloth tabs, wood plaques and scroll ends used selectively. Circles belong to portrait seals, small item badges and formation pads. Do not make every destination a gold circle.

Richness budget:
Let illustration carry the richness. Use one principal material treatment on a component and at most one small accent. A panel need not have a scroll roll, gold frame, embossed crest, glowing edge and corner flourish simultaneously. In the normal scene only the principal action uses a full orange filled face.

All dimensions are proposed design constraints, not claimed industry averages. Use dp for layout and sp for text. The visual reference is a 390×844dp outer Android viewport. Actual operating-system insets are measured by the app. The 24dp top and 24dp bottom in the example merely create a convenient 390×796dp safe-content illustration; do not hardcode those OS heights.

Color tokens:

Ink / primary text: #26323A

Indigo cloth / navigation and backing: #223447
Deep cloth / small recesses: #182735
Leaf green / scenery accent: #647E4F
Roof jade / scenery accent: #477763

Use the darker lacquer under white button labels; the lighter orange can decorate the rim. Check contrast against final surfaces and exported textures. Small body text targets at least 4.5:1 contrast; large display text and meaningful non-text outlines target at least 3:1. A textured background still needs a solid text region. Never use saturation alone to communicate ownership, target, failure or selection.

Typography:
- One bundled readable serif family for location and hero names, for example Source Serif 4 Semibold if available.
- One bundled clean sans family for controls, values and body text, for example Noto Sans.
- Use the same selected pair throughout. Do not switch among fantasy fonts from screen to screen.
- Location title: 24sp / about 30sp line height.
- Hero title: 28sp / about 34sp, at most two lines in its intended name region.
- Panel heading: 18sp / 24sp.
- Body and button labels: 16sp / 22–24sp.
- Supporting labels, building plaques, item names: 14sp / 18–20sp.
- Root navigation labels: 13sp / 18sp.
- Compact noncritical metadata: 12sp minimum at normal scale.
- Currency values: 15–16sp with tabular figures where available.
- Avoid all-caps paragraphs, large letter spacing, glowing type and multiple outlines.
- If a font asset is missing, keep one deliberate fallback and report the missing font in review notes. Do not claim the typography is final.

Spacing:
Use 4, 8, 12, 16, 24 and 32dp spacing steps. Normal screen gutter is 16dp; full-bleed art can reach the edge. Related icon/label gap is 6–8dp. Standard panel padding is 16dp. Separate major sections by 20–24dp. Align name baselines and number columns.

Edges:
Most content panels have 4–8dp corners and a 1dp edge. The button can have a small clipped corner or 6dp radius, a 1dp dark edge and 2dp lower lip. Avoid 20–40dp pill radii for every element. Use shallow shadows in one consistent light direction. Reserve large ornamental frames for rare title/reveal compositions.

Touch-space reservation:
Reserve at least 48×48dp around intended controls, including tiny visible glyphs. Explicitly reserve that space for inert visual controls; removing a callback must not collapse the intended hit area. Enlarged invisible areas must not overlap adjacent targets. Artwork and its native label share one future destination identity.

06 — COMPONENT LIBRARY: DRAW BEFORE PROPAGATING

C01 — Primary action
52dp default height, minimum 120dp width, 16dp horizontal padding; dark lacquer face, subtle upper light, narrow dark bottom lip and cream 16sp label. An optional 20dp leading icon must belong to the same set. Full-width variant fills the content gutter. Prepare normal, pressed, unavailable and focused specimens. A pressed specimen lowers the content 1dp. Limit one dominant action per surface.

C02 — Secondary and text actions
Secondary: paper face, 1dp ink/steel edge, dark 16sp label, same control height. Text action: explicit verb plus restrained chevron and a full intended touch envelope. Cancel must be plainly readable. Destructive action uses danger ink and clear wording, never an unexplained red glyph.

C03 — Account/resource header
56dp normal height. Portrait 40dp within a 48dp envelope; name alongside. Two compact resource groups occupy the right, each an illustrated 18–20dp icon and 15sp value. Normal header uses paper with a thin indigo seam. The sample name is "Konoha Genin"; no invented level number competes with currencies. Long account names truncate after a useful portion, with the full name designed in Profile. Use a two-row 88dp variant when text size or width requires it. No plus badges in the default UI.

C04 — Child header
56dp normal height; Back arrow plus readable destination title; at most one contextual utility on the right. Keep the entire left control distinct from Close. A long title wraps in an expanded header instead of becoming unreadably small. Child headers reuse the materials of C03.

C05 — Building and plaque
Each navigable building is a coherent illustrated object with a visible entrance and quiet clearance around it. A 28–32dp tall paper-on-wood plaque holds a 14sp title. The future touch region includes the building and plaque. Selected specimen adds a restrained edge light or a brighter plaque, not a neon ring around the whole neighborhood. Locked specimen adds a steel padlock and short reason. Show normal, selected and locked appearances.

C06 — Root navigation
Four equal segments in 80dp. Baselines, icon height and label weight are identical across roots. Selected segment uses paper fill and ink icon/text. At 200% text, increase bar height or use a reviewed accessible navigation layout without dropping labels. No center action, fifth review tab or ornamental orb.

C07 — Character card
Three columns in the standard 390dp grid. With 16dp gutters and 8dp gaps, each card is about 114dp wide. Use an approximately 114×146dp art/name unit. The face occupies most of the portrait region; hair and eye line are consistent across the roster. Name band is opaque, up to two lines. Add at most one role/affiliation marker and one status marker. Selected is an outline plus check; in-squad is a small explicit "In squad" badge. Do not cover eyes with stars and levels.

C08 — Item/shop tile
Three columns, about 114×152dp per shop cell at reference size. Art region about 90×84dp, short name below, then currency icon and price on a separate line. A quantity marker belongs to the art corner, not the price. Keep all objects centered to a common optical scale. Sold state retains recognizable art with a paper "Sold" label. Supply a two-column/row alternative at large type.

C09 — Tabs, filters and sorting
Paper or cloth tabs, 44–48dp visual height with intended touch padding. Selected has dark ink plus an orange underline or paper tab lift. Use at most four readable tabs in a row. Filter control includes its label and a count only in the appropriate variant. Long labels wrap or move to a sheet. Do not scroll a five-tab row invisibly with no affordance.

C10 — Resource icon/value row
Ryo coin; tied training scroll for EXP; sealed invitation scroll for recruitment; distinct fractured seal/portrait token for fragments; small labeled container for other established materials. Preserve actual resource identities. A coin, seal and fragment must be distinguishable without color. Amounts sit on a consistent baseline.

C11 — Progress and requirement
A labeled rail with current and target values; do not use a decorative progress fill with no explanation. Requirement rows pair icon, name, required amount and available amount. A deficit includes a written shortage. Pending has its own status, not a full success bar.

C12 — Character stage
Separate background, transparent character art, ground/contact shadow and native labels. Keep at least one quiet side for name or short details. Design focal anchors for face, body and weapon. Portrait and field crops come from appropriate assets. Character art never has a rectangular background accidentally left around it.

C13 — Formation position
Five stable position IDs. Oval ground contact, subtle rim, short position/name caption when needed. Empty is a restrained dashed pad with a plus. Selected uses warm rim plus inspector state. Locked and empty are different. Full-body art sits above the pad, with feet attached to the ground plane. No chess-token circles substituted for fighters.

C14 — Jutsu and status art
Jutsu icons use a square illustrated vignette with one clear subject. Status icons use small, simpler silhouettes. Wound is a cut-mark token; Shield a ward; Silence a crossed voice/seal; Mark a target tag. A tiny status icon never tries to depict a whole battle. Supply a labeled glossary specimen. Do not establish a Naruto character's final skill kit from sample art.

C15 — Data sheet / inspector
Opaque paper, 16dp padding, small overline if needed, 18sp title, image or portrait anchor, a concise explanation, relevant fields and action at the end. Long content scrolls. Keep the selected object visible in both parent and sheet.

C16 — Modal / confirmation
Dim background about 35–45%; opaque panel occupies at most the safe width minus 32dp. Name the action/object, show important cost or consequence, then primary action and Cancel. Tall content scrolls above the actions; never clip controls under the gesture inset. No extra Close if Cancel/Back is the chosen consistent dismissal design.

C17 — Reward / reveal
Small rewards sit on one paper tray, each with an icon and exact amount. First-copy character reveal uses a tall character composition with restrained scroll/chakra framing. Already-collected is quieter. Never decorate an unknown/pending result as confirmed.

C18 — Empty / locked / pending / error
Use a small local illustration, clear heading, short explanation and one next-action specimen. Empty shop uses an empty display tray; empty archive uses a blank folder; empty bench uses the open formation pad. Reuse the layout, not the same irrelevant generic illustration everywhere.

C19 — Badge and notification
Normal approval screens have no badges. A later specimen uses one 6–8dp dot or compact count at the upper corner of its object, never on a face or price. Selected, unread and urgent pending are different states. No permanent pulsing glow.

C20 — Tooltip / glossary note
Short paper callout for one sentence and one icon; larger explanations open a designed full sheet specimen. A tiny question mark has a labeled meaning in the review/accessibility description. Never hide required price or targeting information exclusively in a tooltip.

C21 — List / table / ledger row
48–64dp minimum comfortable row depending on content; portrait/object at left, title and metadata middle, value/status aligned right. Expanded variants stack secondary values. Alternating heavy boxes are unnecessary; subtle rules and grouping suffice.

C22 — Toggle / slider / input specimen
Native-looking but themed controls on quiet paper. Labels and displayed values remain visible. Checkbox uses a tick; radio selection uses a filled center; slider has a clear track/thumb. Input specimens have label, content, helper/error text and visible focus. They remain static, with no credential entry or submission.

C23 — Combat HUD
Compact actor bars, small status row, one active-actor indication, one readable target cue, a short current-event caption and bottom viewing controls. Distinguish HP, shield and Chakra with shapes/labels. Reserve a clean central space for the action. Status numbers do not overlap faces.

C24 — Review atlas
External review frame with screen ID, category, fixture, asset status and preview variant. Show exactly one clean game screen when capturing screenshots. The atlas can switch specimens; it is never a game destination. Include "art pending" and "render unverified" honestly when applicable.

Quality inspection:
Inspect assets at full size for anatomy, cutout halos, melted edges, duplicated windows, broken tools, stray text and inconsistent symbols. Texture discipline:
